Theoretical Background & Sign Conventions

The Thin Lens Equation

For a thin spherical lens, the relationship between the object distance ($d_o$), the image distance ($d_i$), and the focal length ($f$) is defined by the Thin Lens Equation:

\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} \quad \implies \quad d_i = \frac{f \cdot d_o}{d_o - f} Where: distances are measured along the horizontal optical bench relative to the center of the lens.

Magnification Formula

Magnification ($m$) is the ratio of the image height ($h_i$) to the object height ($h_o$). It is calculated using the relative distances:

m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o} A positive magnification ($m > 0$) means the image is upright; a negative magnification ($m < 0$) indicates the image is inverted.

Standard Cartesian Sign Conventions

  • Focal Length ($f$): Positive ($f > 0$) for Convex (converging) lenses; negative ($f < 0$) for Concave (diverging) lenses.
  • Object Distance ($d_o$): Positive ($d_o > 0$) for real objects placed to the left of the lens.
  • Image Distance ($d_i$):
    • Positive ($d_i > 0$): Image is formed on the opposite side of incoming light (right). It is a **Real Image** (can be projected on a screen) and is **Inverted**.
    • Negative ($d_i < 0$): Image is formed on the same side as the object (left). It is a **Virtual Image** (cannot be projected, only viewed through the lens) and is **Upright**.

Principal Ray Construction Steps

To locate the image tip visually, three construction rays are traced from the object's tip:

  1. Central Ray (straight ray): Passes through the center of the lens undeviated.
  2. Parallel Ray: Travels parallel to the optical axis, refracts at the lens, and passes through the far focus (for convex lens) or diverges from the near focus (for concave lens).
  3. Focal Ray: Passes through the near focus (or is directed towards the far focus for concave), then refracts and travels parallel to the axis.
