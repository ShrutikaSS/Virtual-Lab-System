Theoretical Background & Trajectory Formulas

Ideal Projectile Motion (No Air Resistance)

In a vacuum, a projectile experiences only a constant downward acceleration due to gravity ($g$). The horizontal component of velocity remains constant, leading to a parabolic flight path described by:

x(t) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t \qquad y(t) = y_0 + v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2} g t^2 Where: $v_0$ is the initial launch velocity, $\theta$ is the launch angle, and $y_0$ is the starting elevation.

The key parameters for a level-ground launch ($y_0 = 0$) are calculated as:

  • Horizontal Range (R): The total horizontal distance traveled:
    R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}
  • Max Height (H_max): The peak altitude achieved:
    H_{\text{max}} = y_0 + \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}
  • Time of Flight (t_flight): The duration of the projectile in the air:
    t_{\text{flight}} = \frac{v_0\sin\theta + \sqrt{(v_0\sin\theta)^2 + 2g y_0}}{g}

Optimum Launch Angle

For ideal conditions with no air resistance, the horizontal range is maximized when $\sin(2\theta) = 1$, which occurs at $\theta = 45^\circ$.

Effect of Air Resistance (Fluid Drag)

When air resistance is enabled, the projectile experiences a resistive drag force ($F_d$) opposing its velocity vector. The drag force is given by $F_d = \frac{1}{2} C_d \rho A v^2$, where $C_d$ is the drag coefficient, $\rho$ is air density, $A$ is cross-sectional area, and $v$ is speed.

This yields non-linear differential equations solved numerically via Euler Integration:

a_x = -\frac{C_d}{m} v \cdot v_x \qquad a_y = -g - \frac{C_d}{m} v \cdot v_y Where: $v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$ represents the instantaneous speed and $m$ is the mass.

Why drag breaks symmetry: Fluid resistance continuously saps mechanical energy. As a result, the projectile falls more steeply during the latter half of its flight, making the trajectory asymmetric and shifting the optimal range angle below $45^\circ$.
