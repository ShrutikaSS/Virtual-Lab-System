Theoretical Background & Physics Formulas

Conservation of Energy Principle

The total mechanical energy $E$ of a closed system is conserved if only conservative forces (such as gravity) perform work. In physics, mechanical energy is split into two primary components:

  • Potential Energy ($PE$): The energy stored in the skater due to their position relative to the ground. For gravitational potential energy, it is directly proportional to the height $h$.
  • Kinetic Energy ($KE$): The energy of motion. It is proportional to the square of the skater's velocity $v$.

When no non-conservative forces act (friction coefficient $\mu = 0$):

E = KE + PE = \text{Constant} At the peak points of the track, the velocity is zero, and energy is 100% Potential. At the lowest point, height is minimal, and energy is converted to Kinetic, achieving maximum speed.

Work Done by Friction & Thermal Dissipation

Friction is a non-conservative force that opposes sliding motion, converting mechanical energy into thermal energy (heat). The normal force $N$ pressing the skater against the track is:

N = m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta) Where: $\theta$ is the local angle of the track relative to the horizontal, $m$ is the mass, and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

The kinetic friction force is $F_f = \mu \cdot N$, and the work done by friction ($E_{\text{thermal}}$) is the integration of the friction force over the distance traveled:

E_{\text{thermal}} = \int F_f \cdot ds = \int \mu \cdot m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta) \cdot |v| \, dt

With friction active, mechanical energy decreases, and the skater eventually settles at the lowest local valley of the track. The total energy remains conserved, including thermal energy:

PE + KE + E_{\text{thermal}} = E_{\text{initial}} = \text{Constant}

Summary of Core Equations

PE = m \cdot g \cdot h Where $m$ is mass (kg), $g$ is gravity (m/s²), and $h$ is height (meters).
KE = \frac{1}{2} m \cdot v^2 Where $m$ is mass (kg) and $v$ is velocity along the curve (m/s).
