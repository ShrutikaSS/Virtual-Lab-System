Theoretical Background & Formulas

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) of a Pendulum

A simple pendulum is an idealized physical model consisting of a point mass suspended by a massless, unstretchable string. Under the influence of gravity, when the bob is displaced from its equilibrium position, it experiences a restoring force that acts to return it to the center.

Applying Newton's second law along the tangential direction yields the differential equation of motion:

d²θ/dt² + (g / L) sin(θ) = 0

The Small-Angle Approximation

For small angles (where θ is small in radians), we can make the approximation sin(θ) ≈ θ. This linearizes the differential equation to:

d²θ/dt² + (g / L) θ = 0

This is the defining equation of a Simple Harmonic Oscillator. The general solution describing the angular displacement over time is:

θ(t) = θ₀ · cos(ωt) Where: θ₀ is the initial displacement angle, L is string length, g is gravitational acceleration, and ω = √(g/L) is the angular frequency.

Time Period Calculation & Corrections

Under the small-angle approximation, the theoretical period of oscillation T₀ is completely independent of the mass of the bob and the amplitude:

T₀ = 2π · √(L / g)

When the initial angle θ₀ exceeds 20°, this approximation fails. The true period becomes longer, which we estimate using a second-order power series correction:

T = T₀ · (1 + θ₀²/16) Where θ₀ is expressed in radians. In this simulation, the data panel dynamically adjusts to this corrected value to mirror real physical behavior.

Conservation of Energy

In the absence of damping, mechanical energy is conserved. Energy continuously shifts back and forth between:

  • Potential Energy (PE): PE = m · g · h = m · g · L(1 - cos(θ)). PE is maximized at the endpoints (where θ = ±θ₀).
  • Kinetic Energy (KE): KE = ½ · m · v² = ½ · m · L² · (dθ/dt)². KE is maximized at the bottom (equilibrium point, θ = 0).
  • Total Energy (TE): TE = PE + KE = constant. When air resistance is enabled, energy is dissipated as heat, and TE exponentially decays.
