Theoretical Background & Formulas

Strong vs. Weak Acids and Bases

The strength of an acid or base refers to its ability to ionize or dissociate in water. It is not the same as concentration.

  • Strong Acids/Bases: Dissociate completely (approx. 100%) in aqueous solution. For a strong monoprotic acid $HA$, the hydronium concentration is equal to the acid concentration ($[H_3O^+] = C_a$). Examples include $HCl$, $HNO_3$, and $NaOH$.
  • Weak Acids/Bases: Dissociate only partially in water, establishing a dynamic chemical equilibrium. The vast majority of the molecules remain intact as unionized neutral particles. Examples include acetic acid ($CH_3COOH$) and ammonia ($NH_3$).

Dissociation Constants (Ka and Kb)

The equilibrium constants for ionization of weak acids ($K_a$) and weak bases ($K_b$) quantify their relative strengths:

Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻] / [HA] For the reaction: HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻
Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻] / [B] For the reaction: B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻

Solving for Ion Concentration (Quadratic Equation)

To find the concentration of hydronium ions ($x = [H_3O^+]$) generated by a weak acid at initial concentration $C$, we solve: $$K_a = \frac{x^2}{C - x} \implies x^2 + K_a x - K_a C = 0$$ Using the quadratic formula, we extract the physically viable positive root:

x = [H₃O⁺] = (-Ka + √(Ka² + 4 · Ka · C)) / 2 For weak bases, replacing Ka with Kb yields the hydroxide ion concentration: x = [OH⁻].

Percent Dissociation

The percent dissociation ($\alpha$) measures the fraction of acid molecules that ionize, which decreases as the solution becomes more concentrated (Le Chatelier's Principle):

% Dissociation = ([A⁻] / C) × 100% = (x / C) × 100%
