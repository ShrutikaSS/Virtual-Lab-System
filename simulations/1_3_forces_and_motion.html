Theoretical Background & Physics Formulas

Newton's Second Law of Motion

Newton's second law states that the acceleration $a$ of an object is directly proportional to the net force $F_{\text{net}}$ acting on it, and inversely proportional to its mass $m$:

F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a \quad \implies \quad a = \frac{F_{\text{applied}} + F_{\text{friction}}}{m} Where: Net Force is the vector sum of all horizontal forces. Acceleration represents the change in velocity per unit time.

Static vs. Kinetic Friction

Friction is a resistive force acting opposite to the direction of motion or intent of motion. It manifests in two distinct regimes:

  • Static Friction ($f_s$): Operates when the object is stationary. It increases to match the applied force exactly, up to a maximum limit:
    f_s \le f_{s,\text{max}} = \mu_s \cdot N
  • Kinetic Friction ($f_k$): Acts when the object is sliding. It has a constant value that is generally lower than maximum static friction:
    f_k = \mu_k \cdot N

Here, $N = m \cdot g$ is the normal force, representing the weight of the box pressing against the horizontal surface ($g \approx 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).

Friction Material Coefficients

Different materials have distinct surface coefficients, yielding different friction responses:

Surface Static Coeff. ($\mu_s$) Kinetic Coeff. ($\mu_k$)
Ice 0.10 0.05
Wood 0.40 0.30
Carpet 0.60 0.50

If the applied force is less than $f_{s,\text{max}}$, the box does not move. This is demonstrated visually in the simulation: below the threshold, the box will vibrate or shake, indicating that static friction is holding it stuck.
