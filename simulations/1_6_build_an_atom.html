Interactive Lab Challenges

Work through these interactive challenges to verify your understanding of atomic structure:

Challenge 1: Build a Neutral Helium-4 Atom

Drag exactly 2 Protons, 2 Neutrons, and 2 Electrons into their places. Verify that the "Element" reads Helium, the "Mass Number" reads 4, the "Net Charge Card" reads Neutral Atom, and the isotope is marked Stable.

Challenge 2: Construct a Radioactive/Unstable Isotope

Build a Hydrogen atom (1 proton) but drag in 2 neutrons to make Hydrogen-3 (Tritium). Notice how the nucleus starts to vibrate/shake. This represents that the ratio of neutrons to protons is unstable and subject to radioactive decay!

Challenge 3: Create a Positive Lithium-6 Ion

Build a Lithium atom (3 protons, 3 neutrons) but add only 2 electrons. Check the "Net Charge Card" and notice the balance scale tipping to the left (+). It should display Positive Ion, and the symbol notation should show a superscript +1 charge.

The Anatomy of an Atom

Atoms are the basic building blocks of all matter. They are composed of three subatomic particles:

  • Protons (P): Positively charged particles ($+1\text{ e}$) found in the dense center of the atom (the nucleus). The number of protons defines the identity of the element.
  • Neutrons (N): Neutral particles (no charge) found in the nucleus. They add mass to the atom and help bind protons together against electrostatic repulsion.
  • Electrons (e): Negatively charged particles ($-1\text{ e}$) that orbit the nucleus in specific circular shells (Bohr model).

Key Definitions

  • Atomic Number ($Z$): The number of protons in the nucleus. Defines the chemical element (e.g., $Z=1$ is Hydrogen, $Z=6$ is Carbon).
  • Mass Number ($A$): The total number of protons and neutrons ($A = Z + N$).
  • Net Charge: Calculated as $\text{Protons} - \text{Electrons}$. A neutral atom has equal protons and electrons. An ion is formed when they are unequal.
  • Isotopes: Atoms of the same element (same number of protons) but different numbers of neutrons (different mass numbers).
